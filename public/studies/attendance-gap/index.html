01 / THE QUESTION

Attendance improved.
So why is there still a gap?

It is easy to see a falling absence rate and assume the problem has been solved. The longer trend tells a more nuanced story. This study translates national statistics into a clearer picture of scale, differences between groups, and what the data can actually establish.

[ THE CONTEXT ]

The analytical challenge

Education statistics are reported in different units: sessions missed, pupil enrolments, school types and year groups. Without careful definitions, superficially similar percentages can tell very different stories.

This project asks three questions: How has absence changed? Where are the gaps largest? And what can someone do with that knowledge?

THE CENTRAL INSIGHT

Better than last year does not mean back to normal.

In 2024/25, overall absence was still higher than its 2018/19 level despite a year-on-year improvement. Improvement and recovery are different questions.

05 / THE IMPORTANT BIT

What the numbers don't tell us.

A good data analyst doesn't stop at the strongest-looking chart. The interpretation matters just as much as the calculation.

[ THINK CRITICALLY ]
↗

Association ≠ causation

Research finds that school attendance is associated with attainment, even after accounting for several other factors. That does not mean we can infer that each extra day causes a specific grade improvement for any individual pupil.

◎

Missing context matters

Illness alone accounted for 3.22% of possible sessions in 2024/25 — nearly half of all recorded absence. Disability, health, disadvantage and other factors complicate simple comparisons.

⌁

Denominators matter

An overall absence rate counts missed sessions. Persistent and severe absence figures count pupil enrolments crossing a threshold. These measures are related, but cannot be interchanged.

This page is a portfolio data story, not a real-time monitoring dashboard or causal research paper. Figures describe state-funded primary, secondary and special schools in England, with enrolment- and session-based definitions as specified by DfE. THE TAKEAWAY

Good analysis gives context,
not just answers.

Attendance is improving nationally, but the gap to pre-pandemic levels remains. Exploring different measures, backgrounds and definitions helps us ask better questions — and make better decisions.
