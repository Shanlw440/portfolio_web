THE PROBLEM / INVENTORY PLANNING

Too much stock ties up cash. Too little stops the operation.

A conventional inventory report tells you how much is sitting in the warehouse. It may not tell you whether that amount will survive a late supplier delivery, a change in demand or a service-level requirement.

The decision is not simply how much do we have? It is when should we reorder, how much buffer is justified, and which items need attention first?

REAL MATHEMATICS. SYNTHETIC DATA.

NO MAGIC.
JUST MATH.

The interactive visual is the interface. The decisions come from deterministic inventory formulas, with rounding up to whole units. This makes the outputs inspectable and reproducible.

↗ READ THE ENGINE SOURCE
FORMULA / 01Safety stockceil( z × σ_daily × √lead_time )

Buffer for variability assuming independent daily demand and fixed lead time.

FORMULA / 02Reorder pointceil( μ_daily × lead_time + safety_stock )

When on-hand stock falls to or below this threshold, trigger replenishment. No outstanding orders are assumed.

FORMULA / 03Order-up-to levelceil( μ × (L + 14) + z × σ × √(L + 14) )

Our explicitly chosen demo policy: cover the fixed lead time plus a 14-day review period. When triggered, order the gap to this target.

LIMITATIONS / READ BEFORE APPLYING

The calculator is not a probabilistic Monte Carlo simulation, demand forecast fitted to historical data, or live stock ledger. Daily demand averages and standard deviations are invented demo inputs. The simple normal-demand model assumes independent variation, fixed lead time, immediate full receipt on the arrival day, and no planned receipts or minimum order quantities. It does not model lost sales, backorders, seasonality, supplier reliability, case packs or warehouse constraints. The chart is a deterministic expected-demand scenario, not a confidence interval or a guaranteed delivery outcome.
