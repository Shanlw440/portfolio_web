01 / THE QUESTION

Same country.
Different realities.

Census 2021 reported striking variation in population density between local authorities. A percentage can be national; a service decision must still work locally.

The 2021 Census found 15,695 residents per km² in Tower Hamlets and just 26 per km² in Powys. Those places face very different geographic patterns, but density alone cannot tell us which needs more funding.

Consider library access, social care visits, local transport or neighbourhood services. Distance, age, income, demand, staffing and infrastructure all matter. This project asks what we can learn from density, what we cannot, and how simple scenario modelling can help a council frame better questions.

03 / PUT THE NUMBERS IN CONTEXT

Density ≠ demand.

Here is what density can describe, and why it should not be mistaken for a complete service planning model.

Lower density can mean longer distances to services, but the authority average hides settlements, rural roads and population clusters.

Separate data from scenario

Use observed authority-wide densities as inputs. Radius, participation and service-hub capacity are user-selected assumptions, not recorded council outcomes.
